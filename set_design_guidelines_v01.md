# Tridevskoye Tsarstvo (The Thrice-Nine Kingdom)
# MtG Custom Set — Design Guidelines & Rules v0.1

---

## 1. SET OVERVIEW

**Name**: Tridevskoye Tsarstvo (The Thrice-Nine Kingdom)
**Set Code**: TNK
**Size**: ~300 cards (large set)
**Card Distribution**: 101 Common, ~80 Uncommon, ~64 Rare, ~20 Mythic, ~35 Basic Land/other
**Theme**: Russian and Slavic folklore — fairy tales (skazki), heroic epics (byliny), mythology, and selected literary works
**Tone**: Top-down design. Flavor leads, mechanics follow. The set should feel like opening a book of fairy tales, not a reskinned generic fantasy set.

---

## 2. CENTRAL CONFLICT

**"The Old Gods vs. The New Faith"** — The Christening of Rus.

The set's world is defined by the collision between incoming Christianity and the indigenous Slavic pagan system. This is NOT a clean good-vs-evil split. The historical reality was centuries of syncretism (двоеверие / dual faith), and the set reflects that ambiguity. Both sides have heroes and villains; both sides have virtue and cruelty. Neither is "the right side."

### World Geography: Home vs. Outside

The set divides into two spatial categories:

**Home** — where heroes come from:
- Cities (WU): Kitezh Grad, cathedrals, merchants, scribes
- Villages (W, WG): Peasants, fields, harvest, household spirits
- The Druzhina camps (WR): Prince's warband, bogatyr outposts

**Outside** — where heroes venture on quests:
- The Magic Forest (GB): Baba Yaga's realm, spirits, talking animals, trials
- The Sea and Overseas (U, UB): Underwater kingdoms, merchant voyages, remote islands, foreign lands
- The Underworld / Nav (B, BR, BU): The realm of the dead, demons, unquiet spirits
- The Wild Steppe (RG, possibly tricolor): Nomad riders, robbers, dragons, giants

This geography maps onto gameplay: defensive, value-oriented decks come from "home" colors, while "outside" decks are wilder, riskier, and more extreme. A player running Quests is literally sending their creatures from home into the outside.

## 3. COLOR PHILOSOPHY

### White — Fields, Faith, Community
Peasants, pilgrims, vityazi, the village, the new faith's virtues (humility, selflessness, compassion). The "home" color.
- **Mechanics**: Go-wide (Peasant/Soldier tokens), lifegain, quest support ("on a quest" buffs and protection), Nakaz exile/management, enchantments
- **Creature types**: Human Peasant, Human Soldier (Vityaz), Human Cleric, Human Noble
- **Frost aspect**: Endurance, virtue tested by cold (Morozko's test), frozen silence of faith

### Blue — Water, Knowledge, Foresight
Rusalki, Vodyanoy, merchants, townsfolk, scribes, transformation, the sea. The "seeing" color.
- **Mechanics**: Scry (primary scry color, high density), card draw, bounce, transform DFCs, Treasure (merchants)
- **Creature types**: Spirit, Human Merchant, Fish, Bird, Ship (artifact creatures)
- **Frost aspect**: Ice as control and stasis, frozen rivers, ice mirrors, tap-and-don't-untap effects
- **Special**: Scry represents seeing far away / seeing the future (блюдечко с яблочком, magic mirror, prophetic dreams)

### Black — Death, Curses, the Underworld (Nav)
The unquiet dead, Koschei, Baba Yaga's death aspect, witches, demons, Gogol's gothic horrors (Viy, Pannochka). The forbidden and the transactional.
- **Mechanics**: Nakaz (primary generator), sacrifice, graveyard recursion, Morbid, Soulshift, Undying (select cards), Curses
- **Creature types**: Spirit, Zombie, Demon, Human Warlock, Human Rogue
- **Frost aspect**: Winter as death, the killing cold, the dead season, -X/-X as freezing to death

### Red — Fire, Passion, Recklessness
Bogatyri in their hot-blooded aspect, Ivan Durak, Emelya, zmei/dragons, smiths, Ural miners, the bonfire on Kupala Night. Action and audacity.
- **Mechanics**: Haste, Mount/Saddle, aggressive quests, Treasure tokens, combat tricks, quest-completion payoffs
- **Creature types**: Human Soldier (Bogatyr), Human Peasant, Human Artificer (Smith), Dragon, Demon
- **Frost aspect**: Opposition to frost — bonfires, Maslenitsa, forge-heat. But also Morozko himself (capricious, temperamental)

### Green — Forest, Animals, the Old Ways
Leshiy, talking animals, bears, wolves, the oak, the apple tree, the ancient and unchosen. Nature persists.
- **Mechanics**: Big creatures, Food tokens (primary generator), Bestow (helpers), Mount/Saddle (wolves, bears), three-counter patterns, ramp
- **Creature types**: Spirit, Beast, Wolf, Bear, Fox, Bird, Treefolk, Frog
- **Frost aspect**: Hibernation, dormancy, frozen forest that still lives underneath, creatures that "wake up" (three-counter thaw)

### Cross-Color Notes
- **Transform DFCs** are spread across ALL colors, not exclusive to any one. Triggers and flavor change by color (Green: nature transformation; Blue: water/magic; Black: curse; Red: fire/passion; White: faith/redemption)
- **Mount/Saddle** is primarily RG with some White. Green has animal mounts (Grey Wolf, Bear), Red has warhorses and magical steeds (Sivka-Burka, Ognenny Pes), White has war-mounts (Dawn Rider)
- **Food tokens** are generated primarily by Green and White, but appear across all colors with different flavor
- **Frost/Winter** is sprinkled across all colors, not exclusive to Blue

## 5. THEMATIC PRECONSTRUCTED DECK VISIONS

These are aspirational constructed/precon deck identities, not strict draft archetypes:

1. **Kitezh Grad** (UW control): Walls, defensive spells, clerics, merchants, craftsmen, city guards, up to the Tsar. Kitezh Grad itself is a legendary land.
2. **Druzhina** (WR aggro): Warriors, bogatyri, byliny and historical epic (Slovo o Polku Igoreve) flavor. Mounts, quests, combat.
3. **Durak's Quest** (UR or WUR): Combo deck capitalizing on the Quest mechanic. Weaker creatures with large quest-completion benefits, artifacts and helpers. Ivan Durak as the cover/face card. The designer's personal favorite.
4. **Kochevniki** (RG aggro): Steppe nomads, riders, robbers in mythical form. Natural opposition to Druzhina. Dragons, giants, monsters.
5. **Dark Woods** (GB control): Magical forest full of spirits, talking animals, traps and trials. Baba Yaga's realm. Features "inverse Nakaz" (Ispytaniye — spells that give the opponent something in the short term but with a devastating triggered punishment).
6. **Blue Sea** (U midrange): Sea creatures, merchant ships, Vodyanoy, remote islands and countries. Scry-heavy, transformation DFCs.
7. **Chertovshina** (B or BR sacrifice aggro): Gogol-flavored. Unquiet dead, demons (cherti), night terrors. Tricky Nakaz spells, sacrifice synergies.

## 6. ORIGINAL KEYWORD MECHANICS

### 6.1 Quest

**Card type**: Enchantment — Aura Quest

**Rules**:
- Enchant creature you control *(it is on a quest)*.
- Each Quest has one test condition and one reward effect.
- When the test condition is met, the reward resolves and the Quest is sacrificed.
- If the enchanted creature leaves the battlefield, the Quest is sacrificed (no reward).

**Template**:
```
Quest of [Name] {cost}
Enchantment — Aura Quest
Enchant creature you control (it is on a quest)
[Optional: passive buff while attached, e.g. "Enchanted creature gets +0/+2."]
When [test condition involving enchanted creature], [reward effect]. Sacrifice ~.
```

**Key design points**:
- A Quest should capture a MOTIF or ARCHETYPE, not a complete story plot. "Cross the river," "slay the beast," "reach the far kingdom" — not "the full tale of Ivan Tsarevich."
- Test difficulty scales with rarity: easy at common (creature is untapped), demanding at rare (dealt combat damage to a player and destroyed a creature).
- Some Quests have a passive buff while attached (helps the Hero survive to complete the test).
- Quests can optionally resolve at end of turn instead of immediately — representing the hero "returning home with the treasure." This creates a vulnerability window. Not all Quests use delayed resolution; it depends on flavor (fetching something = delayed; enduring something = immediate).

**"On a quest" as a set-wide status**:
- Being "on a quest" is a state that other cards in the set care about.
- Cards throughout the set can buff, protect, or trigger off creatures that are on a quest.
- Examples: "+1/+1 while on a quest," "can't be blocked by more than one creature while on a quest," "whenever a creature on a quest deals combat damage, scry 1."

**"Completing a Quest"**:
- A Quest is "completed" when it is sacrificed as part of its own effect (the test condition was met).
- Cards can trigger on quest completion: "Whenever a creature you control completes a Quest, [effect]."
- A Quest that is sacrificed for any other reason (creature died, enchantment destroyed) is NOT completed — it failed.

**Hostile Quests**:
- Some Quests (primarily in Black and Blue) can enchant an opponent's creature.
- These represent villains sending heroes on deadly errands, or imprisoning them.
- The test condition triggers something bad for the opponent.
- Example: "Enchant creature an opponent controls. When enchanted creature attacks, its controller loses 3 life and sacrifices it. Sacrifice ~."

**Legendary Quests**:
- Named-story Quests (Quest for the Firebird, Quest for Koschei's Death) are Legendary.
- Only one copy in play at a time, but can be replayed after completion.
- Generic motif Quests (Quest of the Grave Vigil) are not legendary.

**Multiple Quests on one creature**: Allowed. The Tsar piling impossible tasks on Ivan is too good a flavor beat to lose. Rewards Voltron-style hero building.

### 6.2 Nakaz (Prohibition / Curse)

**Card type**: Instant or Sorcery with the Nakaz ability word.

**Rules**:
- The spell has an above-rate immediate effect (the "temptation" — powerful for its cost).
- After resolving, it goes to the graveyard with a triggered restriction.
- The Nakaz ability triggers ONCE when you violate the prohibition, then exiles itself.
- "As long as ~ is in your graveyard, whenever [trigger], [punishment]. Exile ~."

**Template**:
```
[Name] {cost}
Instant/Sorcery
[Powerful immediate effect — above rate for the mana cost]
Nakaz — As long as ~ is in your graveyard, whenever [trigger condition],
[punishment effect]. Exile ~.
```

**Key design points**:
- The immediate effect should be ABOVE RATE — more powerful than a normal spell at that cost. The Nakaz is the price for that excess power. The deal must be tempting, like every deal in a fairy tale.
- The Nakaz fires ONCE and exiles itself. It's a time bomb, not a permanent restriction.
- The trigger should be a specific action the player might need to take (casting a spell, attacking, tapping a creature, drawing cards) — NOT something unavoidable.
- The player knows the Nakaz when they cast the spell (it's printed on the card). They're making a conscious bargain.
- Nakaz cards are public information in the graveyard. The opponent can see the restriction and play around it.
- Multiple Nakazy can stack in a graveyard — each with a different trigger. Navigating multiple time bombs is the challenge.
- Nakaz can be removed by exiling the card from the graveyard (graveyard hate = Nakaz removal). White is the primary Nakaz-removal color (absolution, purification).

**Nakaz-management cards**:
- White: "Exile target card with Nakaz from a graveyard" (absolution)
- Black: "Target player triggers all Nakaz abilities of cards in their graveyard" (forcing punishment)
- Blue: "Exile target card with Nakaz from a graveyard. Draw a card." (knowledge of how to lift curses)
- Creatures that grow stronger while Nakaz cards are in your graveyard (living dangerously)
- The WB (Двоеверие) archetype is built around generating Nakaz (Black) and exiling it (White)

**Nakaz is ONLY on instants and sorceries** — the graveyard trigger is what makes it mechanically distinctive. Permanents with drawbacks are just normal MtG design.

### 6.3 Ispytaniye (Trial) — Unnamed/Light Mechanic

**Concept**: "Inverse Nakaz" — spells that give the OPPONENT something beneficial, but with a triggered punishment when they use it in a specific way.

**Example**: "Target opponent creates a 3/3 Beast token. Whenever that token attacks, its controller sacrifices two creatures."

**Design space**: Primarily in the Dark Woods (GB) archetype. The forest offers gifts that are traps. Flavorfully distinct from Nakaz (which punishes YOU for YOUR choices) — Ispytaniye punishes the OPPONENT for taking what you offered.

**Status**: Light mechanic, not a keyword. Appears on a handful of cards, primarily GB.

## 7. REUSED EXISTING MECHANICS

### Primary (high density, set-defining):
- **Transform (Double-Faced Cards)**: Set-wide, all colors. The most important reuse. Triggers vary by color and story (completing a quest, breaking a promise, being cursed, dying, dealing combat damage). Creatures, and occasionally other permanents, that transform.
- **Sagas**: Used selectively. Some cards (like Dobrynya i Zmei) combine Saga structure with Quest mechanics (Saga-Quest hybrid: advances automatically like a Saga, but the named creature is "on a quest" throughout).
- **Food tokens**: High density. Generated primarily by Green and White, but appear in all colors. Flavored as fairy-tale feasts, offerings, the oven's bread. Can be used for lifegain (baseline) or sacrificed for other effects (card draw, damage, resurrection).
- **Mount / Saddle**: Primarily RG with some White. Saddle N means: tap any number of other creatures you control with total power N or more; the Mount becomes "saddled" until end of turn. Mounts are already creatures; saddled Mounts gain additional abilities. Key creatures: Grey Wolf, Sivka-Burka, bears, warhorses, the self-propelled oven, seahorse. A creature on a quest riding a saddled Mount is a core set fantasy.

### Secondary (moderate density, supports themes):
- **Bestow**: Sparingly, primarily Green and White. Reflavored as "helpers" — the magic wolf, the golden fish, the household cat. Either a creature or an Aura that buffs your hero.
- **Soulshift N**: Primarily Green and Black. When a Spirit dies, return a cheaper Spirit from graveyard to hand. Captures the idea that pagan spirits cycle and persist — they're hard to permanently remove. Renamed/reflavored as "Peresozhdenie" (rebirth) is optional.
- **Curses (enchantment subtype)**: Black and Black-adjacent. Enchant-player Auras that punish. Slavic curses (proklyatiye, porcha, sglaz).
- **Devotion**: At uncommon+ as a faith-conflict mechanic. Devotion to White = Christian piety; devotion to Green/Black = connection to old ways. Cards that check comparative devotion encode the двоеверие tension.
- **Scry**: Blue's primary identity mechanic at unusually high density. Represents seeing far away (блюдечко с яблочком), prophecy, the magic mirror. Cards that reward or trigger off scrying.
- **Treasure tokens**: Primarily Red and Blue (UR). Merchant economy, quest rewards, dragon hoards.

### Tertiary (low density, selective use):
- **Morbid**: "If a creature died this turn" — ability word on Black and Black-adjacent cards. Supports the gothic/Nav layer.
- **Undying**: On Koschei and 2-3 other creatures max. Too swingy for high density. The thing that won't stay dead.
- **Adventure**: Selective use for bogatyri and quest-heroes if needed. Creature goes on a journey (spell half) then arrives (creature half).
- **Monarch**: Low density, high flavor. The fairy-tale obsession with who rules. Ivan Durak becoming tsar = becoming the Monarch.
- **Raid**: "If you attacked this turn" — on select Red/White aggressive cards.
- **Flashback**: Select spells that echo the "old ways persisting" theme.

### Explicitly NOT used:
- Ninjutsu, Bushido, Channel (too Kamigawa-associated)
- Mutate (wrong transformation flavor)
- Amass / Army tokens (wrong feel for individually characterized spirits)

## 8. UNNAMED MECHANICAL PATTERNS

### Three-Counter Pattern (Rule of Three)
Not a keyword — a consistent design pattern across the set. Cards that enter with three counters (trial counters, lock counters, acorn counters, bloom counters, ice counters, etc.) and something happens when the last is removed. Removal trigger varies (each upkeep, each attack, each spell cast, etc.). When the last counter is gone, the payoff fires.

Examples: sleeping bogatyr with three slumber counters, sealed chest with three lock counters, frozen lake with three thaw counters, fern flower with three bloom counters.

### "On a Quest" Riders
Cards throughout the set that reference the "on a quest" creature status. These are NOT Quest cards themselves — they're creatures, enchantments, equipment, and spells that reward you for having creatures on quests. This makes the Quest mechanic feel pervasive without every card being a Quest Aura.

### Frost / Winter
A light unnamed pattern spread across all colors — not a keyword. Approximately 8-12 cards across the set reference cold, frost, or winter, each in the color's own idiom. Frost counters, tap/don't-untap effects, freezing-flavored debuffs. Key characters: Morozko (Father Frost), Snegurochka (Snow Maiden), General Moroz.

### Hostile Quests
Quests that enchant opponent's creatures — a pattern, not a separate mechanic. Primarily in Black (the wicked stepmother sending the child to die) and Blue (the Sea King imprisoning visitors).

## 9. CREATURE TYPE TAXONOMY

### Human subtypes (primarily "home" colors W, R, WU, WR):
- **Peasant**: The backbone. White primarily, also Red and Green. Go-wide, humble, numerous.
- **Soldier** (Vityaz): White and Red. Warriors, the Druzhina. Aggressive.
- **Cleric**: White. Priests, monks, pilgrims. Lifegain, Nakaz removal.
- **Noble**: White, Blue, Black. Tsars, knyazi, princesses. Legendary creatures.
- **Merchant** (Kupets): Blue. Traders, ship captains. Treasure, artifacts.
- **Warlock** (Ved'ma/Koldun): Black, Green. Witches, sorcerers. Not "Wizard" — these are boundary-crossers.
- **Artificer** (Kuznets/Smith): Red. Smiths, craftsmen. Artifact synergy.
- **Shaman**: Green, Red. Folk magic practitioners. Food, nature magic.
- **Rogue**: Black, Red. Robbers, tricksters, grave robbers.

### Non-human creature types:
- **Spirit**: The broad type for pagan supernatural beings. Green, Black, Blue. Includes: Leshiy, Vodyanoy, Rusalka, Domovoi, Navka, Mara. Soulshift chains.
- **Beast**: Green. Generic large animals (aurochs, bears when not specifically typed).
- **Wolf**: Green. Talking wolves, mount wolves. Saddle synergy.
- **Bear**: Green. Mount bears.
- **Fox**: Green. Trickster animals.
- **Bird**: Blue, Green, Red. Swans, ravens, the Firebird (Zhar-Ptitsa).
- **Fish**: Blue. Sea creatures, the golden fish, Chudo-Yudo whale.
- **Frog**: Green. Tsarevna-Lyagushka. Transform into Human Noble.
- **Dragon** (Zmei): Red primarily, some Black. NOT Western dragons — shapeshifting, cunning, sometimes multi-headed. Includes: Zmei Gorynych, Tugarin Zmeyevich, Poloz.
- **Demon** (Chert): Black primarily, some Red. Devils, petty demons. Not the MtG "big demon" — many are small tricksters.
- **Zombie**: Black. Upyri (Slavic vampires), risen corpses, the animated dead.
- **Treefolk**: Green. The ancient oak, the apple tree, the viburnum bridge.
- **Cat**: White, Green. Monastery cats, household cats. The Ucheniy Kot (Pushkin's learned cat).
- **Horse**: Red, Green. Sivka-Burka, Konek-Gorbunok. Mount creatures.
- **Construct**: Red. Animated objects — the self-propelled oven, the walking shoe, the self-striking club.

## 10. SOURCE MATERIAL

- Afanasyev's skazki collection (Russian fairy tales)

### Pan-Slavic Expansion (light touch):
- Polish Strzyga, Czech Polednice (Lady Midday), South Slavic Vila
- Broader Slavic creature types where they enrich the bestiary

### Historical/Epic:
- Slovo o Polku Igoreve (The Lay of Igor's Campaign) — flavor for the Druzhina archetype
- Battle of Kulikovo — flavor for military cards
- The Varangians, the trade routes, Novgorod — flavor for merchant/Blue cards

## 15. OPEN DESIGN QUESTIONS

1. **Mythic Rares**: ~20 cards not yet designed. Should Slavic gods (Perun, Veles, Mokosh, Stribog, Dazhbog) be Planeswalkers? Or mythic creatures? Or a new card type?

2. **Kitezh-Grad duplication**: Appears as both a rare enchantment and a legendary land. Pick one design or keep both with differentiated names/roles?

3. **Koschei rarity**: His "virtually unkillable" design (Undying + counter reset) may be mythic-level. Currently rare.

4. **Baba Yaga color identity**: Currently BG. Should she be Jund (BRG)? She has fire associations (glowing skull fence) and Red chaos.

5. **Saga-Quest hybrid**: The Dobrynya i Zmei card is both a Saga and a Quest. Too complex for rare? Mythic instead?

6. **Legendary density in limited**: Currently 4 uncommon + ~16 rare legendaries + mythics = very high. Risk of drawing duplicate legends in draft.

7. **Lyudmila**: Ruslan exists at rare but Lyudmila has no card yet. She should appear — likely WU or mono-W.

8. **Slavic vampire (Upyr)**: Currently just a common zombie. Could be expanded into a proper creature type/subtheme, especially with Gogol horror flavor.

9. **Kolobok**: The living pastry that runs away from everyone. Comedy common? Token type? It's too iconic to skip.

10. **The Tsar as archetype**: Multiple fairy tales feature a foolish/cruel Tsar who sets impossible tasks. Should there be a legendary Tsar creature, and in what color? (Probably mono-W or WR, as the authority that sends heroes on quests.)
